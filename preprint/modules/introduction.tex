\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Modern artificial intelligence research has fragmented into multiple conceptual
lineages, each defined by distinct mathematical assumptions, expressive
structures, and stability behaviors. Geometric operator systems, expressive
phenomenological models, pre-classical continuity frameworks, reversible
narrative systems, and spectral-runtime architectures now coexist without a
shared comparative language. This fragmentation complicates analysis, obscures
lineage-specific invariants, and increases the risk of conceptual collapse when
systems are compared without mathematical safeguards.

Shared-Horizon is introduced as a \emph{neutral documentation membrane} designed
to compare these lineages without collapsing their internal geometries. Rather
than blending or unifying models, Shared-Horizon provides a structured,
lineage-preserving framework that maintains conceptual sovereignty across
operator, expressive, narrative, and spectral systems. The framework is built
around a color-tile mosaic that visually and mathematically distinguishes five
major lineages: Non-Dual Holonomy (NDH), ANIMA expressive phenomenology,
Pre-Classical Continuity (PRECL), Reversible Narrative Ecology (RNE), and
Structural-AI operator geometry.

A key contribution of this work is the correction of the green-tile lineage.
Earlier interpretations treated the lineage as symbolic ambiguity, but analysis
of reversible expressive transitions, curvature preservation, and narrative
geodesics demonstrates that the lineage is properly classified as Reversible
Narrative Ecology (RNE). This correction is mathematically necessary and forms a
central pillar of the Shared-Horizon framework.

The purpose of this preprint is fourfold:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item To define the mathematical foundations of Shared-Horizon using a
    modular LaTeX spine covering operator geometry, expressive manifolds,
    reversible narrative operators, stability envelopes, transition maps, and
    collapse-risk geometry.

    \item To present the color-tile mosaic as a lineage-preserving comparative
    structure that prevents conceptual collapse between expressive, operator,
    narrative, and spectral systems.

    \item To analyze external academic references---including ANIMA Classic
    (Zenodo DOI: 10.5281/zenodo.22812349), the Anima GitHub repository, and NDH
    Meta-Systems analysis---strictly for scholarly comparison and mathematical
    correctness.

    \item To provide a case study demonstrating how Shared-Horizon enables
    lineage-safe comparative analysis using real expressive and narrative data.
\end{enumerate}

This introduction establishes the motivation, scope, and contributions of the
Shared-Horizon preprint. The following modules develop the mathematical spine,
formalize lineage boundaries, and demonstrate how the neutral membrane enables
safe, reversible, and academically rigorous comparison across diverse AI
ecosystems.
