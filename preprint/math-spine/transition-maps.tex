\section{Transition Maps}
\label{sec:transition-maps}

\subsection{Cross-Manifold Transition Maps}

Let $\mathcal{X}$ and $\mathcal{Y}$ be smooth manifolds representing distinct
lineage state spaces (operator, expressive, or narrative). A transition map is a
smooth function
\[
\Psi : \mathcal{X} \rightarrow \mathcal{Y}
\]
used to compare states across lineages without activating either lineage’s
internal dynamics.

\subsection{Smoothness Requirements}

Transition maps must satisfy:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item $\Psi \in C^\infty(\mathcal{X}, \mathcal{Y})$.
    \item $\Psi$ preserves stability envelopes:
    \[
    E_{\mathcal{Y}}(\Psi(p)) \le \epsilon_{\mathcal{Y}}
    \quad \text{whenever} \quad
    E_{\mathcal{X}}(p) \le \epsilon_{\mathcal{X}}.
    \]
    \item $\Psi$ does not induce collapse:
    \[
    \Psi(p) \notin \partial \mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{Y}}.
    \]
\end{enumerate}

These conditions ensure lineage-safe comparison.

\subsection{Local Charts and Compatibility}

Let $(U, \phi)$ be a chart on $\mathcal{X}$ and $(V, \psi)$ a chart on
$\mathcal{Y}$. The chart-level transition map is:
\[
\psi \circ \Psi \circ \phi^{-1} : \phi(U) \rightarrow \psi(V).
\]

Compatibility requires:

\[
\psi \circ \Psi \circ \phi^{-1} \in C^\infty(\phi(U)).
\]

This ensures that transition maps respect the smooth structure of both
manifolds.

\subsection{Jacobian Constraints}

The Jacobian of the transition map,
\[
J_{\Psi}(p) = D\Psi(p),
\]
must satisfy lineage-specific constraints:

\begin{itemize}
    \item Operator $\rightarrow$ Expressive:
    \[
    \| J_{\Psi}(p) \| < C_{\text{op-exp}}
    \]
    to prevent operator curvature from overwhelming expressive curvature.

    \item Expressive $\rightarrow$ Narrative:
    \[
    \det J_{\Psi}(p) \neq 0
    \]
    to preserve narrative reversibility.

    \item Narrative $\rightarrow$ Operator:
    \[
    J_{\Psi}(p) \text{ bounded}
    \]
    to prevent narrative curvature from inducing operator collapse.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Transition Map Families}

A family of transition maps is defined as:
\[
\mathcal{T} = \{ \Psi_1, \Psi_2, \ldots, \Psi_k \}
\]
with each $\Psi_i$ satisfying:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item Smoothness.
    \item Stability preservation.
    \item Collapse-risk avoidance.
\end{enumerate}

Families are used for comparative membrane analysis.

\subsection{Bidirectional Transition Maps}

Bidirectional maps satisfy:

\[
\Psi^{-1} \text{ exists and is smooth}.
\]

Bidirectional maps are required for reversible narrative comparison:

\[
\Psi : \mathcal{N} \leftrightarrow \mathcal{N}'.
\]

They preserve narrative curvature:

\[
K_{\mathcal{N}'}(\Psi(p)) = K_{\mathcal{N}}(p).
\]

\subsection{Transition Map Summary}

Transition maps define lineage-safe comparisons between operator, expressive,
and narrative manifolds. They preserve stability envelopes, respect smooth
structure, and avoid collapse-risk boundaries. These maps form the mathematical
basis for Shared-Horizon’s neutral comparative membrane.
